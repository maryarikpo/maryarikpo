# Faith and Technology

Elder David A. Bednar teaches that strands woven together make one strong rope. For me, my faith and my work in IT are not separate strands. They belong together.

## My three guiding principles

### 1. Service
My skills are gifts meant to bless others. IT is a service profession, whether I am supporting users, protecting data, or fixing a system.

### 2. Faith and trust in the Lord
Technology changes fast, and I will never be finished learning. I learn by study and by faith, one step at a time.

### 3. Integrity
Being trusted with data is a responsibility. I want to do careful, honest work, even when no one is watching.

## Collected materials and reflections

### Stewardship and integrity

**Luke 12:48:** "For unto whomsoever much is given, of him shall be much required."
*My reflection:* Being trusted with something is a responsibility, not just a privilege. In IT, I may have access to private records and systems, so the more access I have, the more careful I need to be.

**Doctrine and Covenants 58:27–28:** "Men should be anxiously engaged in a good cause... for the power is in them, wherein they are agents unto themselves."
*My reflection:* I am responsible for what I do with my agency. I cannot blame tools, people, or circumstances for my choices. As an IT professional, I want to own the results of my decisions about security, testing, and honesty.

**Personal insight: every record belongs to a real person.**
*My reflection:* Researching the 2024 Change Healthcare ransomware attack, I learned that one missing safeguard (multifactor authentication) exposed the private health information of millions of people. Those records are real people's diagnoses, bills, and worries. I want to be the person who speaks up about a small gap before it becomes a big problem.

### Learning and growth

**Doctrine and Covenants 88:118:** "Seek learning, even by study and also by faith."
*My reflection:* I started in health and moved into IT. This verse reminds me that changing direction is okay when I keep learning and keep trusting God.

**2 Nephi 28:30:** "Line upon line, precept upon precept, here a little and there a little."
*My reflection:* Some weeks school feels like too much. This verse reminds me that growth comes in small pieces, and IT skills are built in layers.

**Personal insight: nothing I have learned has been wasted.**
*My reflection:* Customer service and student support taught me patience, accuracy, and how to explain things clearly to stressed people. I now see that experience as something I bring into IT, not something I leave behind.

**Joseph B. Wirthlin, "Come What May, and Love It" (2008).**
*My reflection:* Hard seasons, including months without work, did not stop my growth. Choosing to keep going with a hopeful attitude helped me see how the Lord was preparing me.

**Dieter F. Uchtdorf, "Be Not Afraid, Only Believe" (2015).**
*My reflection:* Linux and databases once scared me. Choosing to believe I could learn them, and then doing the work, turned them into my strongest skills.

### Service and purpose

**Mosiah 2:17:** "When ye are in the service of your fellow beings ye are only in the service of your God."
*My reflection:* A job is not only about a paycheck. When I help someone solve a problem, I am also serving God.

**Matthew 25:40:** "Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me."
*My reflection:* Security failures often hurt ordinary people the most. Protecting their information is one way to care for "the least of these."

**Personal insight: mentoring is a form of discipleship.**
*My reflection:* As a mentor and advisor, I learned that helping others learn helps me understand things better. I want to keep explaining IT in simple, kind language.

## Ethics in IT: what my discussions taught me

- **Change Healthcare (2024):** one missing safeguard led to a massive health data breach. Small gaps matter.
- **Apple's iCloud photo scanning plan (2021–2022):** protecting children and protecting privacy pulled in opposite directions. Good intentions still need careful design.
- **Facebook and Cambridge Analytica (2018):** growth was chosen over consent. Data should be treated as borrowed, not owned.

The common lesson: every technical decision creates a tradeoff, and the most ethical approach is to ask for consent, involve the people affected early, and be honest when plans change.

Elder Gerrit W. Gong's three guideposts, rely on the Spirit, practice wisdom, and choose trusted sources, guide how I want to use AI: checking results against reliable data, protecting privacy, watching for bias, and using AI to support my thinking, not replace it.
